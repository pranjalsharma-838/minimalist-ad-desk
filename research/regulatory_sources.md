# Regulatory Source Base: "Policy & Claims" Dimension (Minimalist ad pre-screen)

Compiled 2026-10-02 by the regulatory-researcher role. This is a **pre-screen aid, not legal advice**. Anything flagged here should go to Minimalist brand/legal for a final call.

**Confidence tags**
- **VERIFIED**: I read the primary text myself today (official site, Gazette PDF, or a government-hosted copy of the statute). Quotes are verbatim, apart from whitespace cleaned up during PDF extraction.
- **SECONDARY**: taken from a reputable summary (law-firm note, standards database, search-result listing). Not read in the primary text.
- **UNVERIFIED**: from background knowledge. Needs a legal check before anyone relies on it.

**Clause numbering.** ASCI clause numbers (1.1-1.8, 3.1-3.6, 4.1-4.4) were confirmed against ASCI's own *Codes & Guidelines Book* PDF. Where the source has no numbers, the ID is descriptive (for example ASCI-G-FAIR) and no clause number is claimed.

**Entry format.** ID · instrument + section · tag. Then the quote (or a marked PARAPHRASE), the URL, and an ad-line test: what the rule means, an example phrase that breaks it, and a compliant rewrite.

## A. ASCI Code for Self-Regulation of Advertising Content (India)

Primary URLs: https://www.ascionline.in/the-asci-code/ and the numbered book https://www.ascionline.in/wp-content/uploads/guidelines/ASCI_Codes_Guidelines_Book.pdf

**1. ASCI-SCOPE** · Code: "Responsibility for the Observance" + Definitions · VERIFIED
> "This Code applies to advertisements read, heard or viewed in India even if they originate, or are published abroad, as long as they are directed to consumers in India..." / "Any written or graphic matter on packaging... is subject to this Code in the same manner as any advertisement in any other medium."
- Means: global creatives shown to Indian users, pack shots and on-pack text are all in scope. Violates: assuming a US-made Reel shown in India is exempt. Rewrite: screen every India-facing asset, including the pack visible in frame.

**2. ASCI-1.1** · Ch. I cl. 1.1 (substantiation) · VERIFIED
> "Advertisements must be truthful. All descriptions, claims and comparisons, which relate to matters of objectively ascertainable fact, should be capable of substantiation."
- Means: every measurable claim needs evidence on file before launch. Violates: "Reduces pigmentation by 63% in 4 weeks" with no study on the finished product. Rewrite: "In a 4-week study of 30 users, 63% saw visibly reduced dark spots*", with the study on file.

**3. ASCI-1.2** · Ch. I cl. 1.2 (research source and date) · VERIFIED
> "Where advertising claims are expressly stated to be based on, or supported by independent research or assessment, its source and date should be indicated in the advertisement."
- Means: "clinically proven" or "lab-tested" needs the source and date in the ad itself. Violates: "Clinically proven to fade dark spots". Rewrite: "Clinically tested: [lab name], [month year], n=__*".

**4. ASCI-1.3** · Ch. I cl. 1.3 (references to persons or institutions) · VERIFIED
> "Advertisements shall not, without permission from the person, firm or institution under reference, contain any reference to such person, firm or institution, which confers an unjustified advantage on the product..."
- Means: naming a dermatology body, hospital or named doctor needs written permission. Violates: "Recommended by AIIMS dermatologists". Rewrite: drop the reference, or hold written consent.

**5. ASCI-1.4** · Ch. I cl. 1.4 (misleading by implication or omission, visuals included) · VERIFIED
> "Advertisements shall not contain statements or visual presentation, which directly, or by implication or by omission or by ambiguity or by exaggeration, are likely to mislead the consumer..."
- Means: retouched skin, filters and implied results count as claims, not just the words. Violates: a heavily filtered "after" face next to the serum. Rewrite: an unretouched result that matches substantiated outcomes, plus "Results vary".

**6. ASCI-1.5** · Ch. I cl. 1.5 (abuse of trust, exaggeration) · VERIFIED
> "Advertisements shall not be so framed as to abuse the trust of consumers, or exploit their lack of experience or knowledge. No advertisement shall be permitted to contain any claim so exaggerated as to lead to grave or widespread disappointment..."
- Means: no promise that most users won't actually get. Violates: "Flawless glass skin in 3 days". Rewrite: "Helps improve skin texture with regular use".

**7. ASCI-1.5(a)-(c)** · Ch. I cl. 1.5 examples ("free", "up to") · VERIFIED
> "(a) Products shall not be described as 'free' where there is any direct cost to the consumer other than the actual cost of any delivery, freight, or postage..." "(c) Claims which use expressions such as 'up to five years guarantee'... are not acceptable if there is a likelihood of the consumer being misled..."
- Violates: "Free sunscreen!" when it needs a ₹999 cart. Rewrite: "Free 30 ml sunscreen on orders above ₹999".

**8. ASCI-1.5(d)** · Ch. I cl. 1.5(d) (perceived physical inadequacy) · VERIFIED
> "Special care and restraint has to be exercised in advertisements addressed to those suffering from... any real or perceived inadequacy of any physical attributes such as height or bust development, obesity, illness... baldness and the like..."
- Means: hair-fall, baldness and body-shape copy gets extra scrutiny. Violates: "Regrow lost hair, end baldness". Rewrite: "Helps reduce hair fall due to breakage*", substantiated.

**9. ASCI-1.6** · Ch. I cl. 1.6 (obvious hyperbole) · VERIFIED
> "Obvious untruths or exaggerations intended to amuse or to catch the eye of the consumer are permissible, provided that they are clearly to be seen as humorous or hyperbolic..."
- Means: only clearly fantastical exaggeration is safe; a claim that sounds plausible is not. Violates: "Your skin will thank you 10x" is borderline. Rewrite: an obviously playful visual ("so good your mirror applauds").

**10. ASCI-1.8** · Ch. I cl. 1.8 (ads on media-company handles, dated July 10, 2025) · VERIFIED
> "Social media advertisements posted on the handles of media companies must be clearly identifiable... adequate disclosures upfront and prominently at the beginning of the post..." Permitted labels: Advertisement, Partnership, Ad, Free Gift, Sponsored, Platform disclosure tags, Collaboration.
- Violates: a paid "skincare myths" post on a publisher's page with no label. Rewrite: open with "Sponsored | Minimalist".

**11. ASCI-CH2** · Ch. II (non-offensive) · VERIFIED
> "Advertisements should contain nothing indecent, vulgar, especially in the depiction of women, or nothing repulsive which is likely... to cause grave and widespread offence."
- Violates: gratuitous sexualised body shots in a body-lotion ad. Rewrite: product-in-use framing.

**12. ASCI-3.1(b)** · Ch. III cl. 3.1(b) (derision) · VERIFIED
> "[No advertisement shall be permitted which] Derides any individual or groups on the basis of race, caste, colour, religion, gender, body shape, age, sexual orientation, gender identity, physical or mental conditions, or nationality"
- Means: no mocking dark skin, ageing, acne-scarred skin or body shape. Violates: "Don't let dull, dark skin hold you back". Rewrite: "Helps even out the look of skin tone".

**13. ASCI-3.3** · Ch. III cl. 3.3 (dangerous practices) · VERIFIED
> "Advertisements shall not, without justifiable reason, show or refer to dangerous practices, or manifest a disregard for safety, or encourage negligence."
- Means: no showing strong acids misused, or sun exposure after a peel without SPF. Violates: "Layer AHA 30% every night for faster results". Rewrite: "Use 1-2x a week; always follow with SPF".

**14. ASCI-3.4** · Ch. III cl. 3.4 (legality) · VERIFIED
> "Advertisements should contain nothing which is in breach of the law, nor omit anything which the law requires."
- Means: a breach of the DCA, DMR or CPA rules below is automatically an ASCI breach too. Example: a drug-type claim on a cosmetic (see DCA entries).

**15. ASCI-4.1** · Ch. IV cl. 4.1 (comparative ads) · VERIFIED
> Comparisons are permissible "provided: It is clear what aspects of the advertiser's product are being compared with what aspects of the competitor's product... The comparisons are factual, accurate and capable of substantiation... The advertisement does not unfairly denigrate, attack or discredit other products, advertisers or advertisements directly or by implication"
- Violates: "Other brands' serums are just water and marketing". Rewrite: "10% niacinamide at ₹X per ml vs Brand Y's 5% at ₹Z per ml (MRP, [date])".

**16. ASCI-4.2 / 4.3** · Ch. IV cl. 4.2 (goodwill), 4.3 (plagiarism) · VERIFIED
> 4.2: "Advertisements shall not make unjustifiable use of the name or initials of any other firm... nor take unfair advantage of the goodwill attached to the trademark..." 4.3: "...shall not be similar to any other advertiser's earlier run advertisements in general layout, copy, slogans, visual presentations, music or sound effects, so as to suggest plagiarism."
- Violates: a "The Ordinary-style" white-label layout that names the competitor in the hashtags. Rewrite: original layout, no competitor tags.

### A2. ASCI Guidelines (https://www.ascionline.in/the-asci-code-guidelines/)

**17. ASCI-G-DISC** · Guidelines for disclaimers · VERIFIED
> "A disclaimer should not attempt to correct a misleading claim made in an Advertisement." / "For comparative claims, the basis of comparison must be stated in a font size that is at least 25% of the font size of the claim..." / "The disclaimer should be restricted to two full length lines and remain on screen for AT LEAST 4 seconds for every line" / HD video: lower-case text "NOT LESS THAN 26 pixels... in a 1080 line raster."
- Means: the pre-screen can check disclaimer size, hold time and language mechanically. Violates: "Visible results in 7 days*" with "*in 2 of 10 users" on screen for 1 second. Rewrite: change the headline itself ("Visible results seen in some users within 7 days*"), and hold the disclaimer for 4+ seconds per line.

**18. ASCI-G-NEW** · Validity of "new/improved" (June 1, 2014) · VERIFIED
> "The words 'new' or 'improved' must specify what aspect of the product/service is new or improved... may be used... only for a period of one (1) year from the time the new or improved product/service has been launched..."
- Violates: "NEW formula!" 18 months after launch. Rewrite: "Improved texture (launched [month year])", used within 12 months.

**19. ASCI-G-FAIR** · Guidelines for skin lightening or fairness improvement products (Aug 14, 2014) · VERIFIED
> "advertising should not directly or implicitly show people with darker skin, in a way which is widely seen as, unattractive, unhappy, depressed or concerned... at a disadvantage of any kind, or inferior, or unsuccessful in any aspect of life..." / "In the pre-usage depiction of product, special care should be taken to ensure that the expression of the models... should not be negative..."
- Means: this applies to tan-removal, brightening and depigmentation creatives (alpha arbutin, vitamin C), not just "fairness" creams. Violates: a sad "before" model with darker skin and a smiling, lighter "after". Rewrite: neutral expressions in both frames, and a focus on spots or even tone ("fades the look of dark spots").

**20. ASCI-G-AWARD** · Guidelines for awards/rankings (Apr 21, 2022) · VERIFIED
> "Advertisements that refer to awards/rankings should indicate clearly the name of the organisation that has provided the award/ranking and the month and year..." / "Awards/rankings should not be used as an alternative for consumer or scientific research or testing, which is required to substantiate a superiority claim..." / "Awards/rankings based on surveys done in one area... cannot be extrapolated to include a larger territory..."
- Means: "No.1", "Best serum" and "Award-winning" need a named source and date, with no extrapolation. Violates: "India's No.1 serum brand". Rewrite: "#1 in Face Serums on [platform], [month year]*", with the data on file (see ASCI-RPT-BPC on leadership claims).

**21. ASCI-G-CELEB** · Guidelines for celebrities (July 13, 2023) · VERIFIED
> Celebrity = paid ≥ "Rs. 40 lakhs" a year for ads, or "social media followership of 500,000 or more". "Testimonials... must reflect genuine, reasonably current opinion... based upon adequate information about, or experience with, the product..." Celebrities must give ASCI "a duly signed written confirmation" of due diligence on request. Celebrities "should not participate in any advertisement of a product... prohibited for advertising under The Drugs & Magic Remedies (Objectionable Advertisements) Act 1954..."
- Violates: a celebrity says "I cleared my acne in a week" with no record that they used the product. Rewrite: a scripted line limited to substantiated claims, with a due-diligence file kept.

**22. ASCI-G-INF** · Guidelines for influencer advertising in digital media · VERIFIED
> "Disclosure is required if there is any material connection between the advertiser and the influencer" (this includes free products, even unsolicited ones). "Disclosure must be upfront and prominent..." Videos ≤15s: label "for a minimum of 3 seconds"; 15s-2min: "1/3rd the length of the video"; ≥2min: the whole section where the brand is discussed. Not "buried in a group of hashtags." Permitted labels: Advertisement, Ad, Sponsored, Collaboration, Partnership, Employee, Free gift, Affiliate. Responsibility sits with the advertiser and the influencer.
- Violates: "#minimalist #skincare #ad" at the end of a caption. Rewrite: an "Ad" overlay in the first seconds, plus "Ad |" at the start of the caption.

**23. ASCI-G-INF-HEALTH** · Influencer Guidelines Addendum II (Mar 6, 2025), Health & Financial influencers · VERIFIED
> Influencers commenting on "technical aspects" must be qualified. This covers "Any prevention, treatment, cure, remedy for medical conditions etc. related claims for Health and Nutrition products or services." They "must have relevant qualifications such as a medical degree..." and disclose them "Superimposed on the visuals prominently and upfront, or mentioned as the opening remark in videos." Unqualified influencers "can share generic information... not in the nature of technical advice."
- Means: an influencer making acne, eczema or condition claims, or a "dermatologist explains" video, needs real credentials disclosed up front. Violates: a lifestyle creator saying "This salicylic serum treats cystic acne". Rewrite: "I like how this feels on my oily skin", or a credentialed dermatologist with the qualification on screen. (Whether skincare counts as "health" is an open question: see Q6.)

**24. ASCI-G-GENDER** · Guidelines on harmful gender stereotypes (June 8, 2022) · VERIFIED
> "...while advertisements may feature glamorous and attractive people, they must not suggest that an individual's happiness or emotional wellbeing depends on conforming to these idealised gender-stereotypical body shapes or physical features."
- Violates: "Clear skin = finally getting the date". Rewrite: benefit-led copy ("lightweight, non-greasy finish").

**25. ASCI-G-DARK** · Guidelines for online deceptive design patterns (May 11, 2023; applicable from Sept 1, 2023) · VERIFIED
> False urgency: "Stating or implying that quantities of a particular product or service are... more limited than they actually are would amount to misleading consumers." Drip pricing: quoted prices "must include non-optional taxes, duties, fees and charges..." Disguised ads: "An advertisement that is of a similar format as editorial or organic content must clearly disclose that it is an ad."
- Violates: "Only 3 left! Sale ends in 10:00" on an evergreen offer. Rewrite: a real end date and real stock, or no urgency claim.

**26. ASCI-G-SGC** · Guidelines for Responsible Labelling of Synthetically Generated Content in Advertising (dated Sept 17, 2026; "come into effect on the expiration of 3 months from the date of their publication") · VERIFIED
> Prohibited even with a label: "Exaggerating product results or features through claims or visual representations to create a misleading impression (e.g., a weightloss product showing a before-after image... through an AI generated image...)" and "Fabricating endorsements or testimonials". A label is mandatory for "synthetically generated influencers and ambassadors". No label is needed for "Routine editing, colour correction, light adjustment, noise reduction, standard blemish removal... that do not alter the substance or core claims of the ad."
- Means: an AI-made "after" skin is banned outright. Blemish removal is "routine" only where blemishes are not the claim, so it is never acceptable in an acne or dark-spot result shot. Violates: an AI-smoothed "after 4 weeks" face. Rewrite: a real, unedited result photo, or no result imagery.

**27. ASCI-RPT-BPC** · ASCI report "Objectionable Ads in the Beauty & Personal Care Category..." (Feb 2023), enforcement pattern, not a rule · VERIFIED
> Violative claim types found: "Ingredient based product efficacy claims where the product itself is not tested to deliver the benefits"; "Claims like 30X or Y% more for better efficacy where the basis of claim was not clear"; "Leadership claims: Such as market share, or No. 1 without adequate data"; "Recommendation by experts: Claims lacking evidence of expert recommendation"; natural/organic claims on mostly synthetic products. Skin care was 30.3% of violating BPC ads, and D2C brands made up 84% of violating BPC advertisers.
- URL: https://www.ascionline.in/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/personal-care-report_compressed.pdf
- Means: this is directly relevant to Minimalist's ingredient-led positioning. Violates: "10% Niacinamide reduces pores by 50%" when the data comes from ingredient literature. Rewrite: "Formulated with 10% niacinamide, an ingredient known to help with the look of pores", or cite a finished-product study.

**28. ASCI-RPT-HEAT** · ASCI press release "The Heat is On" (06 Aug 2026), enforcement pattern · VERIFIED
> "Advertising for sun protection and tan removal products emerged as the largest violative category..." "Instant beauty transformation claims remained widespread... 'De-tan and Glow in 30 Seconds', 'Visible Difference in 5 Days'..." Skin-brightening and tan-removal ads aimed at children "reinforced harmful colour-based stereotypes". 39 of 40 ads reviewed needed modification.
- URL: https://www.ascionline.in/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/Press-Release-The-Heat-Is-On.pdf
- Means: SPF and de-tan creatives are ASCI's current focus, so expect proactive monitoring. Violates: "Remove tan in 2 minutes". Rewrite: "Helps reduce the appearance of tan with regular use*", substantiated.

## B. Drugs and Cosmetics Act 1940 + Cosmetics Rules 2020 (India)

**29. DCA-COS-DEF** · D&C Act s. 3(aaa) · VERIFIED (quoted verbatim in a CDSCO guidance document)
> "Cosmetic means any article intended to be rubbed, poured, sprinkled or sprayed on, or introduced into, or otherwise applied to, the human body or any part thereof for cleansing, beautifying, promoting attractiveness, or altering the appearance, and includes any article intended for use as a component of cosmetic."
- URL: https://cdsco.gov.in/opencms/export/sites/CDSCO_WEB/Pdf-documents/cosmetics/Guidance_doc_cosm_14Sep2018.pdf
- Means: cosmetic claims must stay within cleansing, beautifying, attractiveness and appearance. Violates: "Heals damaged skin barrier". Rewrite: "Helps the skin look and feel smoother and more hydrated".

**30. DCA-DRUG-DEF** · D&C Act s. 3(b) · VERIFIED (PRS-hosted text "with 2005 amendments"; check for later amendments)
> "'drug' includes-- (i) all medicines for internal or external use of human beings... and all substances intended to be used for or in the diagnosis, treatment, mitigation or prevention of any disease or disorder in human beings... (ii) such substances (other than food) intended to affect the structure or any function of the human body... as may be specified... by notification..."
- URL: https://prsindia.org/files/bills_acts/bills_parliament/2007/bill145_20080430145_THE_DRUGS_AND_COSMETICS_ACT_1940_with_2005_amendments.pdf
- Means: "treat, cure, prevent or heal" a disease or disorder (acne vulgaris, eczema, melasma, rosacea, infection) is drug language and risks the product being treated as an unlicensed drug. Violates: "Treats acne and prevents breakouts". Rewrite: "For acne-prone skin. Helps reduce the look of blemishes" (still flagged for legal: Q1).

**31. COS-R36** · Cosmetics Rules 2020, r. 36 · VERIFIED
> "36. Prohibition against false or misleading claims. — No cosmetic may purport or claim to purport or convey any idea which is false or misleading to the intending user."
- URL: https://cdsco.gov.in/opencms/resources/UploadCDSCOWeb/2022/cos_rules/Cosmetics%20Rules%202020.pdf
- Means: the statutory hook for misleading cosmetic claims. It sits in the labelling part, but CDSCO cites it for claims generally (see entry 33). Violates: "100% chemical-free" on a synthetic formula. Rewrite: "Free from added fragrance".

**32. COS-CAT-SUN** · Cosmetics Rules 2020, product-category list (import registration schedule) · VERIFIED
> The category list includes "Sun protection products", "Before and after sun products", "Self-tanning products", "Anti hair loss products".
- Means: in India, sunscreens and anti-hair-loss products are registrable as cosmetics, unlike the US (see FDA-DEF). A cosmetic SPF claim is still subject to substantiation (entry 63, Q4). Violates: n/a. This entry tells the scorer not to flag "SPF 50" as a drug claim for India.

**33. CDSCO-PN-2026** · CDSCO Public Notice COS-12/1/2026-eoffice, dated 18.05.2026 · VERIFIED 2026-10-05 (primary CDSCO copy opened; earlier SECONDARY via a law-firm note)
> PARAPHRASE: CDSCO restated that cosmetics may only be applied as the s. 3(aaa) definition allows, "no cosmetic is permitted to be used in treatment by professionals/individuals", and that "misleading claims on label, use of cosmetics for treatment and application of cosmetic through injection" violate the Act and the Cosmetics Rules.
- URL: https://cdsco.gov.in/opencms/resources/UploadCDSCOWeb/2018/UploadPublic_NoticesFiles/Public%20Notice%20dated%2018.05.2026.pdf (primary; verbatim: "no cosmetic is permitted to be used in treatment by professionals/ individuals")
- Means: the regulator is actively watching "treatment" positioning for cosmetics this year. Violates: "Clinic-grade treatment for melasma at home". Rewrite: "Targets the look of dark spots and uneven tone".

## C. Drugs and Magic Remedies (Objectionable Advertisements) Act 1954 (India)

Source read: government-hosted copy at https://cbfcindia.gov.in/cbfcAdmin/assets/pdf/Drugs.pdf. The canonical India Code PDF timed out; it is listed in entry 37.

**34. DMR-2** · s. 2(a), 2(b) (definitions) · VERIFIED
> "'advertisement' includes any notice, circular, label, wrapper, or other document, and any announcement made orally or by any means of producing or transmitting light, sound or smoke" / "'drug' includes... (iii) any article, other than food, intended to affect or influence in any way the structure or any organic function of the body..."
- Means: "drug" in the DMR is defined broadly by intended effect. A topical product making structure or function claims can fall within it. Violates: "Boosts collagen production" (a structure claim). Rewrite: "Helps skin look firmer".

**35. DMR-3** · s. 3 (prohibited purposes) · VERIFIED
> "...no person shall take any part in the publication of any advertisement referring to any drug in terms which suggest or are calculated to lead to the use of that drug for... (d) the diagnosis, cure, mitigation, treatment or prevention of any disease, disorder or condition specified in the Schedule..."
- Means: this is a criminal statute (s. 7 penalties), and ASCI-G-CELEB bars celebrities from DMR-prohibited ads. Violates: "Cures leucoderma / white patches". Rewrite: do not advertise.

**36. DMR-4** · s. 4 (misleading drug ads) · VERIFIED
> "...no person shall take any part in the publication of any advertisement relating to a drug if the advertisement... (a) directly or indirectly gives a false impression regarding the true character of the drug; or (b) makes a false claim for the drug; or (c) is otherwise false or misleading in any material particular."
- Means: if a product is a drug (or is argued to be one), every misleading claim becomes a DMR offence. Example: "Doctor-approved acne cure" on a cosmetic.

**37. DMR-SCHED** · Schedule (diseases a drug may not claim to prevent or cure) · VERIFIED
> The 54 listed items include: "Cancer", "Leprosy", "Leucoderma", "Lupus", "Tumours", "Obesity", "Form and structure of the female bust", "Stature of persons", "Sexual impotence", "Diabetes".
- Alt URL (canonical): https://www.indiacode.nic.in/indiacode/bitstream/123456789/18563/1/the_drugs_and_magic_remedies_(objectionable_advertisements)_act,_1954.pdf
- Means: skin-relevant hard stops are leucoderma/vitiligo, lupus, leprosy, skin cancer/tumours (watch "prevents skin cancer" in SPF copy) and obesity (body-firming creams). Violates: "SPF 50 prevents skin cancer". Rewrite: "Broad-spectrum SPF 50 PA++++ helps protect against UVA/UVB" (keep any health-education line generic and legal-approved). Acne, pigmentation and hair fall are **not** on the list I read (Q11).

## D. Consumer Protection Act 2019 + CCPA Guidelines (India)

**38. CPA-2(28)** · CPA 2019 s. 2(28) · VERIFIED
> "'misleading advertisement' in relation to any product or service, means an advertisement, which— (i) falsely describes such product or service; or (ii) gives a false guarantee to, or is likely to mislead the consumers as to the nature, substance, quantity or quality of such product or service; or (iii) conveys an express or implied representation which, if made by the manufacturer or seller or service provider thereof, would constitute an unfair trade practice; or (iv) deliberately conceals important information"
- URL: https://consumeraffairs.gov.in/public/upload/files/CP%20Act%202019_1732700731.pdf
- Violates: "Guaranteed results or your skin back" with no refund mechanism. Rewrite: "Love it or get a refund within 30 days (T&C on site)", only if that policy actually exists.

**39. CPA-21** · CPA 2019 s. 21 (CCPA penalties) · VERIFIED
> The CCPA may order an ad discontinued or modified, and may impose on the "manufacturer or endorser a penalty which may extend to ten lakh rupees", rising to "fifty lakh rupees" for each subsequent contravention. It may bar an endorser for "one year", rising to "three years". s. 21(5): no endorser penalty "if he has exercised due diligence to verify the veracity of the claims".
- Means: this is the cost of getting it wrong, and endorsers carry personal exposure. Use it for severity weighting in the scorer.

**40. CCPA-4** · Guidelines for Prevention of Misleading Advertisements and Endorsements, 2022, para 4(1) · VERIFIED
> An ad is valid if "(b) it does not mislead consumers by exaggerating the accuracy, scientific validity or practical usefulness or capability or performance..."; "(d) it does not suggest that the claims made... are universally accepted if there is a significant division of informed or scientific opinion..."; "(f) it ensures that the claims that have not been independently substantiated but are based merely on the content of a publication do not mislead consumers"; "(g) it complies with... any other sector specific law".
- URL: https://ccpa.doca.gov.in/files/CCPA_Notification.pdf (Gazette, 9 June 2022)
- Means: "science-backed" and "clinically proven" carry legal weight, and quoting a journal paper about an ingredient is not proof that the product works (4(1)(f)). Violates: "Science says retinol erases wrinkles". Rewrite: "Retinol is widely studied for the look of fine lines; our 0.3% formula [product study ref]".

**41. CCPA-5** · para 5 (bait ads) · VERIFIED
> A bait ad must "ensure that there is adequate supply of goods... to meet foreseeable demand" and, if demand exceeds supply, "make clear that the stock of the goods or services is limited".
- Violates: a ₹99 hero-SKU ad with stock of 50 units. Rewrite: "Limited stock: 500 units at ₹99".

**42. CCPA-6** · para 6 (surrogate ads) · VERIFIED
> "No surrogate advertisement or indirect advertisement shall be made for goods or services whose advertising is otherwise prohibited or restricted by law..."
- Means: a cosmetic ad must not be a front for prohibited drug or DMR claims (for example a "skin clinic" brand extension). Usually low risk for Minimalist.

**43. CCPA-7** · para 7 ("free" claims) · VERIFIED
> A free claim must not describe goods as "'free'... if the consumer has to pay anything other than the unavoidable cost of responding... and collecting or paying for the delivery" and must "make clear the extent of commitment"; must "not use the term 'free trial' to describe a 'satisfaction or your money back' offer..."
- Violates: "Free trial kit" that needs a non-refundable ₹199. Rewrite: "Trial kit for ₹199, adjustable on your next order".

**44. CCPA-8** · para 8 (children) · VERIFIED
> An ad addressing or using children shall not "(p) be published in any mass media... in respect of... cosmetic products... which are adverse to the physical and mental health of children"; 8(2): "shall not... (a) be such as to develop negative body image in children".
- Means: no teen or child models in acne or "glow" creatives that imply insecurity. Read this together with ASCI-RPT-HEAT on child-focused de-tan ads. Violates: a 12-year-old worried about tan. Rewrite: adult models, no appearance anxiety.

**45. CCPA-11** · para 11 (disclaimers) · VERIFIED
> A disclaimer "shall not contradict the material claim"; "(b) shall not attempt to hide material information..."; "(c) shall not attempt to correct a misleading claim"; "shall be in the same language as the claim"; "the font used in a disclaimer shall be the same as that used in the claim"; a voice-over claim needs the disclaimer "in sync with the voice over".
- Violates: a Hindi VO claim with a tiny English disclaimer. Rewrite: a Hindi disclaimer, same font family, timed with the VO.

**46. CCPA-12** · para 12 (advertiser duties) · VERIFIED
> "(a) all descriptions, claims and comparisons... which relate to matters of objectively ascertainable facts shall be capable of substantiation..."; "(b) ...indicates the source and date of independent research..."; "(d) ...shall not contain statements or visual presentations which directly, or by implication, or by omission, or by ambiguity, or by exaggeration are likely to mislead..."
- Means: this mirrors ASCI 1.1, 1.2 and 1.4, but with statutory force and s. 21 penalties. Use the ASCI examples.

**47. CCPA-13** · para 13 (endorsement due diligence; foreign professionals) · VERIFIED
> "(1) Any endorsement... must reflect the genuine, reasonably current opinion of the individual... based on adequate information about, or experience with, the identified goods..." / "(2) Where, Indian professionals... are barred under any law... from making endorsement in any advertisement pertaining to any profession, then, foreigner professionals of such profession shall also be not permitted to make endorsement..."
- Means: if Indian doctors are barred from endorsing (an UNVERIFIED premise: NMC professional-conduct rules, Q6), a foreign dermatologist cannot endorse in Indian ads either. Violates: "Recommended by a US board-certified dermatologist" in an India ad. Rewrite: "Formulated with dermatologists' input" (only if true and documented, and still a legal check).

**48. CCPA-14** · para 14 (material connection) · VERIFIED
> "Where there exists a connection between the endorser and the trader... that might materially affect the value or credibility of the endorsement... such connection shall be fully disclosed..."
- Statutory backing for ASCI-G-INF. Violates: an undisclosed gifted-product review. Rewrite: an "Ad" or "Gifted" label up front.

**49. CCPA-DP** · Guidelines for Prevention and Regulation of Dark Patterns, 2023 (Gazette 30 Nov 2023), Annexure · VERIFIED
> "False Urgency" means "falsely stating or implying the sense of urgency or scarcity so as to mislead a user into making an immediate purchase... including (i) showing false popularity of a product..." / "Disguised advertisement" means "posing, masking advertisements as other types of content such as user generated content or new articles..."; for seller content, "the responsibility of making the disclosure... shall be on such seller or advertiser."
- URL: https://ccpa.doca.gov.in/files/The%20Guidelines%20for%20Prevention%20and%20Regulation%20of%20Dark%20Patterns,%202023_1732707717.pdf
- Violates: "10,000 people are viewing this serum right now!" (fabricated), or a UGC-styled ad with no label. Rewrite: real, verifiable counts, or none, and a "Sponsored" label.

## E. Meta Advertising Standards

**50. META-PA** · Ad Standards > Privacy Violations and Personal Attributes (changelog Jun 27, 2024) · VERIFIED
> "ads must not contain content that asserts or implies personal attributes. This includes direct or indirect assertions or implications about a person's... age... physical or mental health (including medical conditions)..." Meta's own examples: allowed "Our new lotion and creams fight wrinkles like never before!"; not allowed "Ready to upgrade your skin to look younger?" and "Do you have diabetes?". Ads can "Use 'you/your' language without a personal attribute."
- URL: https://transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/objectionable-content/privacy-violations-personal-attributes/
- Means: the most common Meta rejection for skincare. "You" plus a condition is unsafe. Violates: "Struggling with your acne?" or "Your dark circles are showing". Rewrite: "A 2% salicylic serum for acne-prone skin" or "Meet our under-eye cream for the look of dark circles".

**51. META-HW-INFERIOR** · Ad Standards > Health and Wellness (changelog Jul 23, 2026) · VERIFIED
> "Ads can't: Promote... cosmetic products or procedures that: Contains statements of inferiority about physical appearance (i.e., terms, descriptions, or questions that are negative and attack an individual's appearance, specific body parts, or hygiene)."
- URL: https://transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/restricted-goods-services/health-wellness/
- Violates: "Embarrassed by your oily, pore-filled face?" Rewrite: "Oil control that lasts all day".

**52. META-HW-WHITEN** · Health and Wellness · VERIFIED
> "Ads can't... Promote skin whitening or bleaching products that cause permanent skin color change."
- Means: depigmentation creatives should be framed as "spots / even tone", never "whitening". Violates: "Permanently lighter skin". Rewrite: "Fades the look of dark spots".

**53. META-HW-BA-AGE** · Health and Wellness (age gating and before/after) · VERIFIED
> "Ads promoting or marketing cosmetic products, procedures or surgeries, must be targeted to people at least 18 years or older." When targeting 18+, advertisers can run "General cosmetic products, procedures, surgeries depicting before and after transformation." The age restriction does not apply to "Non-permanent cosmetic and beauty products such as creams, makeup, hair products..."
- Means: before/after is allowed on Meta. To be safe, run before/after creatives at 18+ even for creams, because the policy wording is ambiguous (Q7). Indian law (ASCI-1.4, ASCI-G-SGC) still governs whether the image itself is truthful.

**54. META-HW-CLAIMS** · Health and Wellness (clickbait, incurable diseases) · VERIFIED
> "Employ clickbait tactics in a health... context, such as sensational language with exaggerated or extreme claims, or promises of specific outcomes within a set timeframe without disclaimers or qualifiers." It also bans claims to "cure, heal, or eliminate" a listed set including "Psoriasis" and "Cancer" (claims about "treating or managing symptoms" are exempt).
- Violates: "Clear skin in 7 days, guaranteed" or "Heals psoriasis". Rewrite: "Many users saw smoother texture in 4 weeks* (*results vary; study ref)".

## G. International (brief)

**58. FDA-DEF** · US FD&C Act s. 201(i) / 201(g)(1), via FDA "Is It a Cosmetic, a Drug, or Both?" (content current 09/11/2024) · VERIFIED
> Cosmetic: "articles intended to be rubbed, poured, sprinkled, or sprayed on... for cleansing, beautifying, promoting attractiveness, or altering the appearance". Drug: "articles intended for use in the diagnosis, cure, mitigation, treatment, or prevention of disease" and "articles (other than food) intended to affect the structure or any function of the body". Drug-making claims include "restore hair growth, reduce cellulite... increase or decrease the production of melanin (pigment) in the skin, or regenerate cells." "...moisturizers and makeup marketed with sun-protection claims are cosmetic/drug products." "The FD&C Act does not recognize any such category as 'cosmeceuticals.'"
- URL: https://www.fda.gov/cosmetics/cosmetics-laws-regulations/it-cosmetic-drug-or-both-or-it-soap
- Means: in US ads, "reduces melanin production", "regenerates cells", any SPF claim, and hair-regrowth claims all make the product a drug. Violates (US): "Alpha arbutin inhibits melanin production". Rewrite (US): "Helps reduce the look of dark spots".

**59. FDA-OTC-ACNE-SPF** · US OTC drug monographs (acne; sunscreen) · VERIFIED 2026-10-05 (eCFR opened; correction: sunscreen labelling is 21 CFR 201.327, acne is 21 CFR 333 Subpart D)
> PARAPHRASE: in the US, topical acne products (for example salicylic acid 0.5-2%, benzoyl peroxide) and sunscreens are OTC drugs governed by FDA monographs (formerly 21 CFR 333 Subpart D for acne and 21 CFR 352 for sunscreens; now OTC monograph orders under the 2020 CARES Act reform). They need Drug Facts labelling, and only monograph-permitted claims are allowed (for example "Broad Spectrum SPF [value]", with "water resistant (40/80 minutes)" only when tested).
- Means: a US-targeted "Salicylic 2% fights acne" or "SPF 50" ad is a drug ad. It must match the monograph-compliant US label. Violates (US): "Sweat-proof SPF 50". Rewrite (US): "Broad Spectrum SPF 50, water resistant (80 minutes)", if tested.

**60. EU-655-1** · Reg. (EU) 655/2013, Art. 1 + Annex §1 (legal compliance) · VERIFIED
> Art 1: applies to claims "in the form of texts, names, trademarks, pictures and figurative or other signs... irrespective of the medium". Annex 1(1): "Claims that indicate that the product has been authorised or approved by a competent authority within the Union shall not be allowed." 1(3): "Claims which convey the idea that a product has a specific benefit when this benefit is mere compliance with minimum legal requirements shall not be allowed."
- URL: https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32013R0655
- Violates (EU): "EU-approved formula" or "Free from banned ingredients". Rewrite: drop both.

**61. EU-655-2/3** · Annex §2 (truthfulness), §3 (evidential support) · VERIFIED
> 2(2): "Ingredient claims referring to the properties of a specific ingredient shall not imply that the finished product has the same properties when it does not." 3(1): "Claims... shall be supported by adequate and verifiable evidence..." 3(6): "A claim extrapolating... ingredient properties to the finished product shall be supported by... demonstrating the presence of the ingredient at an effective concentration."
- Means: this is the EU equivalent of ASCI-RPT-BPC's ingredient-efficacy concern. Violates: "Ceramides repair the barrier, so this cream repairs your barrier". Rewrite: "With 3 essential ceramides at [x]%; in a [n]-person study, skin felt more hydrated after 2 weeks".

**62. EU-655-4/5/6** · Annex §4 (honesty), §5 (fairness), §6 (informed decision-making) · VERIFIED
> 4(1): "Presentations of a product's performance shall not go beyond the available supporting evidence." 4(2): "Claims shall not attribute to the product concerned specific (i.e. unique) characteristics if similar products possess the same characteristics." 5(1): Claims "shall not denigrate the competitors, nor shall they denigrate ingredients legally used." 6(1): "Claims shall be clear and understandable to the average end user."
- Means: "paraben-free because parabens are toxic" denigrates a legal ingredient, and "the only serum with 10% niacinamide" breaks the uniqueness rule. Rewrite: "Fragrance-free; suitable for sensitive skin" (if tested).

**63. BIS-SPF** · BIS IS 17494:2025 (ISO 24444:2019, MOD), in-vivo SPF test method (India) · VERIFIED 2026-10-05 on the BIS e-sale store (Edition 2025, active, 0 amendments); not made mandatory by the Cosmetics Rules (see Q4)
> PARAPHRASE: the Indian Standard specifies the in-vivo determination of SPF and is a modified adoption of ISO 24444:2019. Whether it is mandatory for SPF claims was not confirmed in primary text.
- URL: https://standardsbis.bsbedge.com/ (search "IS 17494"); https://cosmetic.chemlinked.com/ (database entry)
- Means: SPF numbers in India-facing ads should be backed by IS 17494 / ISO 24444 in-vivo testing on the finished product. Violates: "SPF 50" backed only by in-vitro or supplier data. Rewrite: keep the SPF value matched to the in-vivo report on file (Q4).

## Open questions for Minimalist legal

These are genuinely ambiguous. The pre-screen should route matching lines to "needs legal review" rather than auto-pass or auto-fail them. Example wording is taken from product-page titles seen on beminimalist.co today (for example "for Blemishes, Acne Marks", "Fights Breakout & Blackheads", "Tan Removal", "SPF 50... PA++++, Water & Sweat Resistant").

- **Q1. "Acne" on a cosmetic in India.** D&C Act s. 3(b) catches products for "treatment, mitigation or prevention of any disease or disorder", and acne vulgaris is a dermatological disorder. Acne is **not** in the DMR Schedule. Where is the line between "for acne-prone skin" (an audience descriptor), "reduces acne marks" (appearance), "fights breakouts" (arguably mitigation) and "treats acne" (a drug claim)? Has CDSCO, a State FDA or ASCI ruled on salicylic-acid cosmetic claims?
  - **Answered 2026-10-04 by brand/legal: yes, acne wording is acceptable on a cosmetic** (`rules/brand_decisions.json`, DEC-01). Audience, appearance and "fights / reduces / clears breakouts" wording is now advisory. Still flagged: "treats / cures acne" (drug claim, CLM-01), "prevents / stops" (the statute's own word), and elimination promises ("acne-free", "no more acne").
- **Q2. Structure/function language.** "Boosts collagen", "repairs barrier", "exfoliates" and "regulates sebum" may fall within the DMR s. 2(b)(iii) "structure or any organic function" definition. Which verbs does legal accept for India and for the US?
- **Q3. Depigmentation and tan claims.** Does ASCI-G-FAIR apply to "tan removal", "brightening" and "dark spot" SKUs (alpha arbutin, vitamin C, tranexamic acid)? For the US, FDA treats "decrease the production of melanin" as a drug claim. Is a "fades the look of" phrasing acceptable in both markets?
- **Q4. SPF/PA/water-resistance substantiation.** Is BIS IS 17494:2025 (in-vivo SPF) mandatory or only best practice? Which method backs "PA++++" (ISO 24442?) and "Water & Sweat Resistant" (ISO 16217/18861?), and do the claims need a stated duration? Given ASCI-RPT-HEAT, should every SPF creative carry the test reference?
- **Q5. Ingredient-percentage claims.** Minimalist leads with "Niacinamide 10%" and similar. ASCI-RPT-BPC and EU-655 3(6) object to ingredient efficacy presented as product efficacy. Which SKUs have finished-product studies? Lines without one should use "formulated with" wording only.
- **Q6. Dermatologist and "expert" endorsements.** Does skincare count as "Health" under ASCI-G-INF-HEALTH Addendum II? Do NMC professional-conduct rules bar Indian doctors from endorsing (which would trigger CCPA-13(2) for foreign doctors too)? This premise is UNVERIFIED. Is "dermatologist-tested" a factual claim (needs a test report) or an endorsement?
- **Q7. Before/after imagery.** Meta permits before/after for "general cosmetic products" when targeting 18+ but exempts "creams" from age gating. Should we age-gate anyway? Under ASCI-G-SGC (effective around Dec 2026), "standard blemish removal" is routine editing, but in an acne or spot result shot it alters the core claim. Confirm a zero-retouch policy for result imagery.
- **Q8. "Clinically proven" vs "clinically tested".** No ASCI cosmetics-specific guideline was found. The standard comes from ASCI 1.1/1.2 and CCPA 4(1)(b)/12(b). What minimum study design does legal require (sample size, duration, independent lab, CTRI registration)?
- **Q9. Leadership claims.** Which data sources does legal accept for "India's No.1 / bestselling serum": Nielsen, marketplace rank, or internal sales? ASCI-G-AWARD forbids extrapolating from one platform or region to "India".
- **Q10. Labelling rule vs advertising.** Cosmetics Rules r. 36 sits in the labelling chapter. Does legal treat it as applying to e-commerce listing copy and ads? CDSCO's 2026 notice suggests a broad reading (SECONDARY).
- **Q11. DMR Schedule amendments.** I could not confirm whether the 2020 draft DMR amendment (which, from memory, proposed adding conditions such as fairness of skin, premature ageing and greying of hair) was ever notified. If it was, it would directly hit anti-ageing and brightening claims. UNVERIFIED; needs a check.
- **Q12. Cross-border creatives.** The same creative may run in India, the US and the EU. US acne/SPF ads are drug ads (FDA-OTC, UNVERIFIED detail). EU forbids "free from [legally used ingredient]" denigration. Should the tool score per market, or apply the strictest market's rule?
